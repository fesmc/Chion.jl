Accumulation And Melt

Accumulation and melt are handled by the internal helpers _apply_accumulation_resolved! and _melt_surface_layers!, which are called from step!. They are documented here because they define the model behavior, but the intended public entry point is step!.

Accumulation

With snowfall rate $P_{\mathrm{snow}}$ and rainfall rate $P_{\mathrm{rain}}$ in $\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$ and timestep $\Delta t$ in seconds, the added masses are

\[\Delta m_{\mathrm{snow}} = P_{\mathrm{snow}}\Delta t, \qquad \Delta m_{\mathrm{rain}} = P_{\mathrm{rain}}\Delta t.\]

If the column is empty, a new surface layer is created only when snowfall is positive. Rain alone does not create a snow layer, and rain on bare ice (or on a remnant layer below a trace threshold) runs off. Snow falling on a previously bare surface takes $\min(T_{\mathrm{air}}, T_0)$ in every new layer.

Fresh-Snow Density

The scheme is selected with the BESSIModel keyword fresh_snow_density. With fresh_snow_density=:constant (the default), the code uses rho_s (315 kg m⁻³) clamped to [50, rho_i].

With fresh_snow_density=:parameterized, the code uses the HTESSEL formulation (Dutra et al., 2010)

\[\rho_\mathrm{fresh} = a + b\,(T_\mathrm{air} - T_0) + c\,\sqrt{V},\]

with default coefficients rho_s_a = 109 kg m⁻³, rho_s_b = 6 kg m⁻³ K⁻¹ and rho_s_c = 26 kg m⁻⁷ᐟ² s¹ᐟ², wind speed V in m s⁻¹ (5 m s⁻¹ without wind forcing), and a final clamp to $[50, \rho_i]$.

If snowfall is added to an existing surface layer, the new bulk density is mixed by conserving layer volume:

\[\rho_1^{new} = \frac{m_1^{old} + \Delta m_{\mathrm{snow}}} {m_1^{old}/\rho_1^{old} + \Delta m_{\mathrm{snow}}/\rho_\mathrm{fresh}}.\]

Rainfall is added directly to the surface liquid-water store mass_w[1] when a snow layer exists.

Layer-Structure Enforcement

After mass addition, the code:

  1. splits the surface layer when mass[1] > mass_max
  2. frees space at the bottom when all Ntot layers are already active, by merging the two deepest layers and exporting any density excess above $\rho_i$ to the ice
  3. merges or rebalances the surface when mass[1] < mass_min; with near-surface layer limits (the default) this minimum is not applied, because the remeshing controls the surface layers
  4. applies the snow-depth cap described on the Layer Structure And Basal Transfer page
  5. remeshes the four uppermost layers to near_surface_layer_max_thicknesses_m (see Near-Surface Layers)

Melt

If the energy solve holds the surface at the melting point, the energy available for melt is the difference between the atmospheric flux at $T_s = T_0$ and the heat conducted into the uppermost layer,

\[E_{\mathrm{melt}} = \Delta t\left[Q_{\mathrm{net}}(T_0) - G_s\left(T_0 - T_1^{n+1}\right)\right]\]

(see Energy Balance). _melt_surface_layers! consumes this energy from the surface downward. Because a melting layer can be colder than $T_0$, each kilogram costs $L_m + c_i (T_0 - T_i)$ to warm and melt, so the melt mass of layer $i$ is $E/[L_m + c_i (T_0 - T_i)]$. The melted mass is added to the layer's liquid water, and the later retention and refreezing decisions are left to go_percolation! and go_refreezing!.

  • layers that melt completely are removed, and their liquid water is passed to the layer below
  • melt energy left after all snow is gone melts the underlying ice: it lowers smb_ice and runs off directly
  • after melt, the interface temperature Tsrf is set to $T_0$

If melt is prescribed (prescribed_melt forcing), the melt mass is taken directly from the forcing and removed by _apply_melt! at latent heat only. On bare ice, melt follows from the same energy balance with the top ice-substrate layer as the surface (see Layer Structure).

Interaction In step!

The accumulation-and-melt-related ordering in one call to step! is:

  1. accumulation, removal of trace layers, and near-surface remeshing (or the bare-ice branch if no snow is left)
  2. albedo update
  3. densification
  4. go_energy_flux!
  5. vapour exchange (sublimation/deposition) on the surface layer
  6. melt when melt energy is available, or the prescribed melt when prescribed_melt forcing is supplied
  7. go_percolation!
  8. HTESSEL liquid-water compaction when enabled
  9. go_refreezing!
  10. near-surface remeshing and the final albedo update

The full sequence is listed on Model State And Step Flow.

_apply_accumulation!(..., snowfall_rate, rainfall_rate, dt_seconds; air_temperature=nothing, wind_speed=...)

Mutates the supplied state arrays in-place and may also update albedo, runoff, and basal-mass diagnostics. Returns the enthalpy of the ice exported to the ice reservoir (relative to liquid water at T₀).

source
Chion._melt_surface_layers! — Function
_melt_surface_layers!(..., idx, mass_split, mass_min, melt_mass, c, charge_sensible_heat)

Melt surface snow of column idx and convert it to liquid water, removing or merging depleted layers. melt_mass is the melt potential in kg m⁻² of ice at the melting point (the melt energy divided by Lm).

With charge_sensible_heat = true (melt computed from the surface energy balance), ice taken from a layer below T₀ first has to be warmed to T₀, so each kilogram costs Lm + ci (T₀ − T) and the melted mass is smaller than melt_mass. This keeps the column energy budget closed. With charge_sensible_heat = false (prescribed melt), exactly melt_mass is melted.

Returns (melted, remaining): the melted snow mass and the melt potential left when the snow is exhausted (in kg m⁻² of ice at T₀).

source
Chion._apply_melt! — Function
_apply_melt!(..., idx, mass_split, mass_min, melt_mass, c)

Convert up to melt_mass of surface snow into liquid water in column idx (prescribed-melt semantics: no sensible-heat charge) and return the melted mass. See _melt_surface_layers!.
