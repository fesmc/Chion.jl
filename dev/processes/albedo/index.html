Albedo

Surface albedo is stored in state.albedo[idx] and is updated before the energy solve. The energy solver then uses it for the absorbed shortwave radiation $Q_{sw} = (1-\alpha)\,Q_{\mathrm{sw,down}}$.

The scheme is chosen with the BESSIModel keyword albedo:

albedoBehaviour
:dynamic (default)prognostic BESSI albedo with temperature aging, wetness and gradual snowfall brightening
:constantalpha_dry, alpha_wet or alpha_ice from the surface state
:agingexponential decay with time since snowfall (Hoang et al., 2025)
:semixtwo-band SEMIX snow albedo from grain size, dust, solar zenith angle and clouds
:prescribedprescribed_albedo forcing for every column and step
ParameterDefaultMeaning
alpha_dry0.81dry (fresh) snow albedo
alpha_wet0.70wet (aged) snow albedo
alpha_ice0.40bare-ice albedo
max_lwc_albedo0.1surface liquid-water content at which the albedo reaches alpha_wet
aging_cold_timescale_days20:aging timescale below $T_0$
aging_melting_timescale_days2:aging timescale at $T_0$

The defaults are calibrated against MAR over Greenland. The Antarctic paper runs use alpha_dry=0.87, alpha_wet=0.82.

In the formulas below, $T_1$ is the temperature of the first snow layer (temperature[1]), not the interface temperature Tsrf.

Constant Scheme

When albedo = :constant, the code uses:

  • alpha_ice when no snow is present
  • alpha_wet when snow is present and the surface layer is at or above T0
  • alpha_dry otherwise

Dynamic Scheme

The dynamic scheme is the BESSIModel default. It keeps the surface albedo between alpha_wet and alpha_dry; bare ice uses alpha_ice. The defaults alpha_dry = 0.81, alpha_wet = 0.70, and alpha_ice = 0.40 were calibrated against MAR over Greenland in coupled runs.

The dynamic scheme follows the BESSI implementation of Zolles and Born (2021), with temperature-dependent aging rates based on broadband albedo observations (Aoki et al., 2003). The previous albedo is first clamped to [alpha_wet, alpha_dry]. A column that was bare ice ($\alpha = 0.40$) therefore starts from at least alpha_wet once any snow covers it.

Temperature Aging

For an existing snow surface, the code applies

\[\alpha^\star = \min\left( \alpha^n,\; \alpha^n - \Delta t_{\mathrm{days}} \left(1.35\times 10^{-3}(T_1 - T_0) + 0.0278\right) \right),\]

followed by a clamp to alpha_wet, so warm snow darkens faster than cold snow. Scaling by the elapsed time $\Delta t_{\mathrm{days}}$ keeps the aging rate consistent when a forcing step is divided into diurnal substeps.

Wetness Adjustment

The surface liquid-water content is diagnosed from the current pore volume:

\[\mathrm{LWC}_{\mathrm{surf}} = \frac{m_w / \rho_w}{m/\rho - m/\rho_i}.\]

When LWC_surf > 0 and max_lwc_albedo > 0, define r = clamp(LWC_surf / max_lwc_albedo, 0, 1). The wetness adjustment is

\[\alpha^{n+1} = \alpha_{\mathrm{wet}} + (\alpha^\star - \alpha_{\mathrm{wet}})(1-r)^{\Delta t_{\mathrm{days}}}.\]

This preserves the original one-day adjustment and makes wetness relaxation independent of subdivision at fixed wetness, in isolation from temperature aging. Zero elapsed time applies no wetness adjustment; r = 1 reaches alpha_wet for any positive elapsed time. Combined aging, snowfall, and evolving column conditions can still introduce timestep dependence.

Snowfall Refresh

Fresh snowfall brightens the surface according to

\[\alpha^{n+} = \min\left( \alpha_{\mathrm{dry}},\; \alpha^n + (\alpha_{\mathrm{dry}} - \alpha_{\mathrm{wet}}) \left(1 - e^{-\Delta m_{\mathrm{snow}}/3}\right) \right),\]

where Δm_snow is the newly added snowfall mass in kg m^-2. Small events brighten the surface gradually, whereas events of more than about 10 kg m⁻² restore an albedo close to that of fresh snow. The refresh happens during accumulation. The :constant and :aging schemes instead reset the albedo to alpha_dry after any snowfall.

Aging Scheme

Set albedo=:aging to use a snowfall-age albedo following Hoang et al. (2025). Snowfall resets state.snow_age_days to zero and the albedo to alpha_dry. During snow-free forcing intervals the age advances by dt_days, and the albedo relaxes towards the aged-snow value step by step,

\[\alpha^{n+1} = \alpha_{\mathrm{wet}} + (\alpha^n - \alpha_{\mathrm{wet}}) \exp\left(-\Delta t_{\mathrm{days}}/\tau\right),\]

which for constant $\tau$ is the published $\alpha = \alpha_{\mathrm{firn}} + (\alpha_{\mathrm{fresh}} - \alpha_{\mathrm{firn}})\exp(-t/\tau)$ with $t$ the time since snowfall.

The scheme reuses the existing albedo constants: alpha_dry is the fresh-snow albedo, alpha_wet is the aged-snow/firn asymptote, and alpha_ice is used without surface snow. The aging timescale defaults are aging_cold_timescale_days=20 and aging_melting_timescale_days=2. The cold timescale is used below T0; the melting timescale is used at or above T0. A column without surface snow uses alpha_ice and has zero snow age. For time-varying surface temperature, the exponential decay is applied incrementally over each forcing interval. This preserves the published law when the timescale is constant and prevents snow from becoming brighter when the surface returns below T0. Unlike the constant scheme, reaching the melting point does not cause an instantaneous albedo jump.

The existing albedos and the two aging timescales can be overridden through BESSIModel keywords. For example:

model = BESSIModel(grid;
    albedo=:aging,
    alpha_dry=0.82,
    alpha_wet=0.60,
    alpha_ice=0.40,
    aging_cold_timescale_days=20.0,
    aging_melting_timescale_days=2.0,
)

SEMIX Scheme

albedo=:semix uses the spectral snow albedo of SEMIX (Willeit et al., 2024). It computes visible and near-infrared albedos for direct and diffuse light from

  • the snow grain size, which grows with temperature and shrinks with snowfall (semix_snow_grain_fresh, semix_snow_grain_old, semix_snow_0, semix_snow_1, semix_dT_age, semix_f_age_t)
  • the dust concentration in the snow, from optional dust_deposition forcing and amplified during melt of the seasonal snowpack (semix_dust_con_scale, semix_w_snow_dust, state w_snow_max)
  • the cosine of the solar zenith angle (coszm forcing, or the daily mean from latitude and season)
  • the subgrid orographic roughness (z_sur_std forcing; Dang scheme only)

following Dang et al. (2015) (semix_snow_albedo=:dang, default) or Warren and Wiscombe (1980) (:warren_wiscombe). The bands are combined into a broadband albedo with the cloud fraction (cloud forcing) and the visible fraction semix_frac_vu. Over bare ice the scheme uses alpha_ice, or the optional prescribed_ice_albedo forcing.

Prescribed Scheme

albedo=:prescribed takes the surface albedo from the prescribed_albedo forcing, which must be present for every column and step. It also applies over bare ice.
